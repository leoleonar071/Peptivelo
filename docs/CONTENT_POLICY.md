# Content and verification policy

## Source hierarchy

The numbered-reference pilot requires a claim-level source map, per-page stable numbering and working return anchors. Each reference distinguishes label/supplier information from academic research, explains support and limits, records what was actually inspected and gives commercial context. Academic notes include available bibliographic identifiers, study design, population/duration and disclosed conflicts; never claim full-text review or independent integrity clearance when only an abstract or publisher page was inspected. Original arithmetic and editorial interpretation must not borrow citations as if those sources measured the invented example. See `CITATION_PILOT.md` for the current three-page scope.

Manufacturer US labels and retailer pages support label facts, sizes and observed prices. Peer-reviewed primary studies, trial registries and regulators support scientific or regulatory statements. Generative AI, Reddit, marketing copy and competitor scores do not establish clinical facts. Source-backed does not mean independently tested. Each record retains source IDs and consultation dates; future per-field reviews and change history are modeled separately.

## Catalog facts

Preserve exact label names. Distinguish full INCI lists from selected ingredients; the initial catalog deliberately records selections. Never infer concentration from ingredient order. A blend percentage is not an individual active percentage. Brand-disclosed numbers remain attributed disclosures. Do not silently map differently named copper peptide forms to the same ingredient. No clinical recommendation is inferred from either name or price.

## Price observations

Require a price, currency, exact size, source, observation date and verification date before normalizing. Record immutable observations and verify the US market. Availability can be unknown independently of price. Review observations older than 30 days; display actual dates and warn that prices may change. Do not redate a record without revisiting its source. NIOD’s price is absent because the retrieved content did not clearly associate a price with the 30 mL variant.

## Editorial review

Factual label profiles can publish after source checking, provided they make no unsupported efficacy claims. Original editorial comparisons require human review before indexing; the initial comparison remains visibly draft. No reviewer or review dates are invented. Future clinical interpretation requires a qualified scientific reviewer, actual identity, documented scope and primary evidence. Keep cosmetic ingredients, molecules, medicines, indications, trials and regulatory events separate, including jurisdiction-specific status.

## Commercial independence

No active affiliate relationships. Ordinary retailer links are functional and labeled; compensated links require a real authorized affiliate URL, `rel="sponsored"` and a visible disclosure near the relevant links. Review the [FTC Endorsement Guides Q&A](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) before activating programs. Compensation must not change conclusions or ranking. No false reviews, certifications, testimonials or approvals.

## Restrictions

No unapproved-drug sales or affiliate referrals, injection protocols, self-dosing tools, personalized hormone advice, invented safety classifications, or disease-treatment promises. This calculator measures cosmetic cost per unit of volume only. No medical content should be automatically published. Scientific expansion requires qualified human review, current primary evidence and regulatory checks.

## Corrections

Accept a documented correction containing record, market, variant, changed fact and primary source. Preserve historical observations and create a change record when persistent editorial tooling is implemented. Until then, commit the correction with a descriptive version-control history. Operator contact must be supplied before launch.
